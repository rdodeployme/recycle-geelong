What happens after you tip

Landfill transfers the problem. Recycling transforms the outcome. Here is where the things you drop off actually go.

After you tip

You drop off a mattress

It leaves as baled steel

Every mattress is dismantled on site, in about 38 seconds on average. The springs are pulled out, compressed and baled.

99%of the steel recovered. About 30% of a mattress is steel.
You drop off polystyrene

It leaves as a dense block

Polystyrene is up to 98% air. Our densifier presses it out, so three shipping containers of foam become one block about the size of a washing machine.

98%air, pressed out on site

It leaves as a bale

Clean, dry cardboard is flattened and baled on site, then sent for paper-making.

Where it goes

90+ streams are separated on site. These are the ones people bring in most.

Mattresses

Dismantled on site. About 30% of a mattress is steel, and 99% of that steel is recovered and baled. Foam, fabric and timber are separated.

Polystyrene

Densified on site. Expanded polystyrene is up to 98% air, so a mountain of foam becomes a dense block that can be made into new products.

Cardboard

Flattened, baled on site and sent for paper-making.

Whitegoods

Gas and oil are removed safely, then the steel and other metals are separated for recycling.

Metal

Separated and sent for metal recycling.

E-waste

Handled to the AS/NZS 5377 e-waste standard. Metals, plastics and circuit boards are separated for recovery.

Paint

Collected through Paintback, the national paint stewardship program.

Oils

Collected at the waste oil station and sent for re-refining.

Tyres

Kept separate and sent for tyre recycling.

Gas bottles and extinguishers

Degassed safely, then the steel is recycled.

Batteries

Kept separate at the battery station and sent for recycling.

Clothing and fabric

Kept separate for textile reuse and recovery.

Green waste

Kept separate from general waste for organics processing.

Building materials

Separated by material so bricks, concrete, timber and metal can be recovered.

Furniture

Good pieces go to charity and community partners through our Freecycle program. The rest is dismantled so the timber, metal and fabric can be separated.

General rubbish

Sorted for anything recoverable. What is left is shredded to about a quarter of its volume before it goes to landfill. Landfill last.

03

Six reasons for landfill last

01

Less goes in the ground

Every item recovered is one less in landfill, which extends the life of the sites we have.

02

Fewer raw materials

Recovered steel, paper and plastics reduce the need to mine, log and drill for new ones.

03

Lower emissions

Organic waste in landfill releases methane. Recycling also generally takes less energy than making from scratch.

04

Local jobs

Recycling creates more jobs per tonne than landfill does.

05

Less roadside dumping

Affordable, local drop-off gives people a better option than the side of the road.

06

Better for the future

Diverting waste today means cleaner air, water and land later.
